# Papert Lab
## Motivation
In 1962, Douglas Engelbart outlined his vision of "Augmenting Human Intellect," a framework for using computers to facilitate complex problem solving. Similarly, Seymour Papert recognized that computers necessitated a dramatic change in traditional education, such as using computers to develop projects that elicit "powerful ideas." This vision of computing and learning, however, has been largely misinterpreted and underexplored, with most institutions viewing computers as a means of "democratizing" existing (failed) learning paradigms.

Many efforts to use AI to support this vision of computing are misguided and misaligned with the past 50 years of cognitive and learning science research. The connection between learning science and AI systems is not merely analogous: understanding how humans learn enables the development of better intelligent systems. Papert Lab solves nontrivial problems in learning science and artificial intelligence through a cognitive science lens, fostering the relationship among computers, researchers, and students that Engelbart and Papert envisioned over half a century ago.

## About
Papert Lab is an applied learning science lab that investigates the impact of AI on learning, memory, curiosity, and intelligent tutoring systems.

Currently, Papert Lab investigates problem specification in AI-assisted programming and the role of software testing in facilitating high-level, computational thinking.

Papert Lab was founded in 2025 by Hudson Mitchell-Pullman, an independent researcher and high school student. Hudson collaborates with and is mentored by David H. Smith, Annapurna Vadaparty, Qianou Ma, among others.
