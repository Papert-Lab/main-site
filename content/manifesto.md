In 1962, Douglas Engelbart outlined his vision for "Augmenting Human Intellect," a framework for using computers to facilitate complex problem solving. Similarly, Seymour Papert recognized that computers necessitated a dramatic change in traditional education, such as using computers to develop projects that elicit "powerful ideas." This vision of computing and learning, however, has been largely misinterpreted and underexplored, with most institutions viewing computers as a means of "democratizing" existing (failed) learning paradigms. 

Many efforts to use AI to support this vision of computing are misguided and misaligned with the past 50 years of cognitive and learning science research. The connection between learning science and AI systems is not merely analogous: understanding how humans learn enables the development of better intelligent systems. Papert Lab solves nontrivial problems in learning science and artificial intelligence through a cognitive science lens, fostering the relationship among computers, researchers, and students that Engelbart and Papert envisioned over half a century ago.
